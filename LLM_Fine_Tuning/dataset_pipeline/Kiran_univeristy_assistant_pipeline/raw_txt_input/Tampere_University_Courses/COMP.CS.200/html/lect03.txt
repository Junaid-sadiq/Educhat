It's measured in pixels per inch (ppi). Higher ppi values generally indicate sharper images. Printing: for reasonable print quality, a resolution of around 256 ppi is considered acceptable. Displays: typical displays have resolutions ranging from 72 to 100 ppi due to the viewing distance. image dimensions (pixels): In everyday terms, resolution often refers to the image size, expressed as the number of pixels in width and height. example: an 8-megapixel camera might capture photos with a resolution of 3840 x 2160 pixels impact of resolution on screen: increasing resolution typically only enlarges the image without necessarily improving sharpness in print: increasing resolution can enhance detail and sharpness (up to a certain point), but it can also increase the printed size Resize for speed Dimensions of an image measured in pixels for example, the 8 megapixel camera photo when displayed, its physical size depends on the used resolution on paper dimensions ~ 34 x 23 cm on display, dimensions ~100 x 67 cm lots of wasted information being uploaded to a web page the image can be scaled (responsive images) desired size on web page both HTML and CSS can be used for that (next slides) Scaling the image with attributes Layout with CSS, but image size can be defined with the attributes width and height Size of an image is a layout (formatting) feature, why this is allowed? This meant requiring users to download specific codecs or plugins to play videos, creating a fragmented viewing experience. Today, ensuring compatibility across browsers requires a multi-format approach: multiple video formats: videos are often encoded in at least two formats to cater to a wider range of browsers. Popular choices include: MP4 (H.264 + AAC): widely supported and efficient, but may have licensing restrictions. WebM (VP8/VP9 + Vorbis/Opus): open-source and royalty-free, with strong support in modern browsers. Ogg (Theora + Vorbis): Another open-source option, sometimes used in conjunction with WebM. Browsers often leverage video codecs built into the operating system or hardware for playback. This simplifies content embedding from services like YouTube or Maps, which often provide ready-to-use snippets with multiple video formats.
