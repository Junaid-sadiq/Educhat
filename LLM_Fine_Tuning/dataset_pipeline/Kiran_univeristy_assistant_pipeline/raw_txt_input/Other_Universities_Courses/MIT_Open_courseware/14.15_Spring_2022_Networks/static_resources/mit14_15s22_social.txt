In this exercise, we look at how a DeGroot learner might calibrate the weight to assign to her own news as opposed to the news and opinions of those in her social media network. Suppose there is a single knowledgeable agent (node 1) and two DeGroot agents (nodes 2 and 3). In this exercise, we formally show that a regulator who censors only a fraction of misinformation may make the overall spread of misinformation worse. Let us consider two islands of equal size N/2: a left-wing island where all agents have prior belief bi = 1/3 (that θ = R) and a right-wing island where all agents have prior belief bi = 2/3 (that θ = R). There is an article of reliability score r with likelihood of being truthful φ(r) and likelihood of being misinformation 1−φ(r). Recall that an article that is truthful produces a message m = θ with probability p > 1/2 and an article that contains misinformation produces a message m = θ with probability q ≤ 1/2. Let πi be the posterior belief of agent i that the article is truthful (does not contain misinformation) given reliability score r and right-wing message m = R. a. Write the expression for πi. Suppose each agent i decides between taking the action ai = S (“share”) and ai = D (“dislike”).
